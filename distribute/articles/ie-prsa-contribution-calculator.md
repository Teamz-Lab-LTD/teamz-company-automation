A Personal Retirement Savings Account (PRSA) is Ireland's flexible personal pension — available to employees, self-employed, and those not in an occupational scheme. Contributions receive income tax relief at your marginal rate (20% or 40%), but the maximum you can contribute is capped as a percentage of net relevant earnings — and that percentage depends entirely on your age.

## Age-Related PRSA Contribution Limits

Revenue allows higher contribution percentages as you get older, recognising that older workers have fewer years to accumulate a pension:

| Age | Max % of net relevant earnings |
|-----|-------------------------------|
| Under 30 | 15% |
| 30–39 | 20% |
| 40–49 | 25% |
| 50–54 | 30% |
| 55–59 | 35% |
| 60+ | 40% |

The absolute maximum earnings that the percentage applies to is €115,000 (2026). So at 60+, the maximum tax-relieved contribution is €46,000/year.

**Example: Age 47, salary €80,000**
- Age-related limit: 25% × €80,000 = €20,000
- Relief at 40% marginal rate: €8,000 tax back from Revenue
- Effective cost of €20,000 contribution: €12,000 out of pocket

## Employee PRSA Matching Contributions

Employers can also contribute to an employee's PRSA. Since Finance Act 2022, employer contributions to PRSAs are not treated as a benefit-in-kind for the employee, and employers get a corporation tax deduction. The employer contribution doesn't count toward the employee's age-related limit but does count toward the overall €115,000 earnings cap for combined relief.

## The Standard Fund Threshold (SFT)

The total pension fund at retirement must not exceed €2 million (SFT). Contributions beyond this trigger a 40% chargeback. This mainly affects those with very long high-income careers, but it's worth tracking.
