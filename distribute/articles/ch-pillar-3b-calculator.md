# Swiss Pillar 3b Explained: The Flexible Pension Most Expats Overlook

If you live in Switzerland, you've probably heard of Pillar 3a — the tax-deductible private pension with a CHF 7,258 annual cap (2026). But what about Pillar 3b?

Pillar 3b is Switzerland's **unrestricted** private pension. No contribution limit. No withdrawal restrictions. No lock-in until retirement. It's essentially a structured savings vehicle with specific tax advantages — particularly for life insurance products.

## 3a vs 3b: Key Differences

| Feature | Pillar 3a | Pillar 3b |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Contribution limit | CHF 7,258/year | **Unlimited** |
| Tax deduction | Yes (full) | No (except some cantons for insurance 3b) |
| Withdrawal | Only at retirement (with exceptions) | **Anytime** |
| Wealth tax | Exempt during accumulation | **Taxable as wealth** |
| Capital gains (insurance) | Taxed at withdrawal | **Tax-free after 10+ years** |

## Who Should Use Pillar 3b?

1. **You've maxed your 3a** — the natural next step for additional retirement savings
2. **Self-employed** without a Pillar 2 pension fund — 3b adds flexibility
3. **You need liquidity** — 3a is locked, 3b isn't
4. **Life insurance buyers** — 3b life insurance products offer tax-free capital gains after 10 years of holding
