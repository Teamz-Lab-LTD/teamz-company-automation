# P11D Benefits in Kind: How Much Tax Do You Actually Pay on Company Perks?

That company car, private medical insurance, or interest-free loan? HMRC treats them as taxable income. The P11D form reports these "benefits in kind" (BIK), and the tax bill can be surprisingly large.

## Company Car Tax 2025/26

The BIK rate on company cars depends on CO2 emissions and fuel type:

- **Electric (0g/km)**: 2% of list price — still the lowest rate in 2025/26
- **Plug-in hybrid (1-50g/km)**: 2-14% depending on electric range
- **Petrol/diesel (51-170+g/km)**: 15-37% of list price

For a £45,000 electric company car, the BIK taxable value is just £900 (2%). A higher-rate taxpayer pays £360/year in tax. The same car as a petrol model at 130g/km would cost £5,400 in BIK — costing £2,160 in tax.

## Other Common Benefits

- **Fuel benefit**: flat rate of £27,800 × appropriate percentage
- **Private medical**: taxed on the full premium cost
- **Beneficial loans**: interest charged at 2.25% (official rate) on loans over £10,000

## P11D Deadline

Employers must file P11D forms by **6 July** following the end of the tax year. Many employers now "payroll" benefits, meaning the tax is deducted monthly through PAYE rather than reported on P11D.
