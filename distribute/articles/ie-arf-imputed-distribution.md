An Approved Retirement Fund (ARF) is a post-retirement investment account that lets you keep your pension pot invested instead of buying an annuity. Sounds great — but Revenue has a catch: whether you actually withdraw money or not, they assume you take out a minimum amount each year and tax it accordingly. This is the imputed distribution rule.

## The Three Imputed Distribution Rates

Revenue applies the imputed distribution as a deemed withdrawal from your ARF, taxed at your marginal rate:

- **4% per year:** ARFs where the ARF holder is under 71 AND total ARF/AMRF value is under €2 million
- **5% per year:** ARFs where the ARF holder is 71 or over, OR where the value is over €2 million (and under €2.06 million — the graduated band)
- **6% per year:** ARFs valued at €2 million or more in the graduated band

**Example: ARF value €400,000, age 68**
- Imputed distribution: 4% × €400,000 = €16,000 deemed
- If you actually withdrew €16,000+, no additional deemed distribution charge
- If you withdrew €8,000 only: Revenue deems €16,000 → income tax + USC on €16,000
- At 40% marginal + 8% USC: effective charge of ~€7,680 on the €8,000 "shortfall"

## Why This Matters for ARF Drawdown Strategy

If your ARF is growing faster than 4-6%, your imputed distribution acts as a floor on how much you "must" take. Many retirees prefer to at least withdraw the imputed amount to avoid the deemed charge — especially since taking the money gives you the cash to pay the tax.

Withdrawals above the imputed amount are taxed on top, PAYE-style through your ARF provider.

## AMRF Rules Were Abolished in 2022

Prior to 2022, you were required to first fill an Approved Minimum Retirement Fund (AMRF) of €63,500 before accessing the ARF freely. AMRFs were abolished from 1 January 2022 — all existing AMRFs converted to ARFs automatically.
