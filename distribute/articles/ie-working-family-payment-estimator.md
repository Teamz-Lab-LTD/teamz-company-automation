Working Family Payment (WFP) is a weekly tax-free payment for employees with children whose family income falls below a threshold. It was formerly known as Family Income Supplement. The calculation formula is straightforward — 60% of the gap between your actual income and the income threshold for your family size — but the thresholds change yearly and many eligible families never claim.

## The 60%-of-Gap Formula

WFP = 60% × (Income threshold for family size – average weekly family income)

**Example: 2 children, average weekly family income €550**
- 2026 threshold for 2 children: €755/week
- Gap: €755 − €550 = €205
- WFP = 60% × €205 = €123/week
- Annual: €6,396 — tax-free

**Example: 4 children, weekly income €700**
- Threshold for 4 children: €855/week
- WFP = 60% × (€855 − €700) = €93/week

The minimum WFP is €20/week. If your income exceeds the threshold, you receive nothing.

## What Counts as Family Income?

Family income includes: wages of both spouses/partners, social welfare payments (except some specific ones), maintenance payments received. It excludes Child Benefit, Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance, and certain Carer's payments.

## Work Requirement

At least one parent must work at least 38 hours per fortnight (not 19 hours per week — the fortnight basis matters for part-time shift workers). Self-employment counts.

Once awarded, WFP is paid for 52 weeks at the same amount regardless of income changes — you reapply each year.
