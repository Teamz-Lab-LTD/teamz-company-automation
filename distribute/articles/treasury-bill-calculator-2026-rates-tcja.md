## Why T-Bills Beat HYSAs in High-Tax States

The headline 4.30% discount rate on a 13-week T-bill looks worse than a 4.50% HYSA. It is not. Two reasons.

First, the T-bill discount rate is quoted on a 360-day basis against face value. The investment yield (bond-equivalent yield) is calculated against the price you actually paid, on a 365-day year. So a 4.30% discount rate is closer to a 4.41% real yield.

Second, T-bill interest is exempt from state and local income tax. For California, New York, Oregon, and Massachusetts residents, that adds 0.3 to 0.5 percentage points to the after-tax yield versus a CD or HYSA at the same rate.

## TCJA Sunset 2027 — What It Means for T-Bill Investors

This is the part most investors miss. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act expires December 31, 2027. Federal brackets revert to 2017 levels:

- Top bracket: 39.6% (up from 37%)
- 22% bracket: becomes 25%
- 12% bracket: becomes 15%

T-bill interest is fully federally taxable. So the after-tax yield drops under the new brackets — but the state-tax exemption stays. The after-tax gap versus CDs and HYSAs actually widens for high-tax-state residents.

Action: investors holding T-bills with 2027-2028 maturities should consider rolling into 2026 maturities to lock current bracket treatment. Or shift toward Roth IRA-held investments where the tax shield is permanent.

## T-Bill Ladder Strategies

A 4-week T-bill ladder generates weekly liquidity — buy a new 4-week bill every Tuesday, and after one cycle you have a bill maturing each Thursday. This setup beats a savings account by 30 to 50 basis points after state taxes for high-tax-state residents.

A 13-week ladder using $50,000 across four bills purchased one month apart yields the higher 3-month rate but still releases $12,500 every 4 weeks for any need.

For longer horizons, mix 26-week and 52-week bills to capture the steeper part of the curve when long rates exceed short — confirmed by checking the [compound interest calculator](https://tool.teamzlab.com/evergreen/compound-interest-calculator/) at expected reinvestment yields.
