An account-based pension (ABP) must pay out a minimum amount each financial year — set as a percentage of your account balance at 1 July. Withdraw less than the minimum, and your fund loses its tax-exempt status for that year. Withdraw more and you can't put the excess back. Getting the drawdown rate right every year is a core retirement planning task.

## Minimum Drawdown Rates by Age

The ATO sets minimum pension factors that increase with age:

| Age | Minimum drawdown |
|-----|-----------------|
| Under 65 | 4% |
| 65–74 | 5% |
| 75–79 | 6% |
| 80–84 | 7% |
| 85–89 | 9% |
| 90–94 | 11% |
| 95+ | 14% |

Note: During COVID years (2019–20 to 2022–23), the ATO temporarily halved these rates. They've since reverted to standard rates.

**Example: Account balance $600,000 at 1 July, aged 70**
- Minimum drawdown: 5% × $600,000 = $30,000/year = $2,500/month
- If you draw less than $30,000, the pension ceases to be in pension phase

## Why Drawdown Rate Matters for Tax

Income earned inside an account-based pension is tax-free when the fund is in pension phase. This is the primary reason Australians convert super to pension — the 15% accumulation tax disappears. But if you miss the minimum drawdown, that exemption is lost for the year, and you may need to re-validate the pension.

## Drawdown Strategy: Sequence of Returns Risk

Taking large draws in years when markets are down depletes the base faster, leaving less to recover. Many advisors recommend drawing only the minimum in poor market years and more in strong years. This requires knowing the minimum precisely each July.
