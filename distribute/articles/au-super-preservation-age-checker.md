You can't access your superannuation whenever you like. The ATO sets a preservation age — the minimum age at which you can access super on a condition of release. And contrary to what many people think, it's not the same for everyone: it depends on your date of birth, and ranges from 55 to 60.

## Preservation Age Table

| Date of birth | Preservation age |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Before 1 July 1960 | 55 |
| 1 July 1960 – 30 June 1961 | 56 |
| 1 July 1961 – 30 June 1962 | 57 |
| 1 July 1962 – 30 June 1963 | 58 |
| 1 July 1963 – 30 June 1964 | 59 |
| After 30 June 1964 | 60 |

Anyone born after June 1964 — the vast majority of current workers — has a preservation age of 60.

## Reaching Preservation Age Doesn't Mean Unrestricted Access

Reaching preservation age alone doesn't give you full access. You still need a condition of release:
- **Transition to Retirement (TTR):** Once you reach preservation age, you can start drawing a TTR income stream (minimum 4%, maximum 10% per year) while still working. Tax treatment depends on age — over 60 the income is tax-free.
- **Full access:** Available once you reach preservation age AND permanently retire, OR once you turn 65 (regardless of employment).
- **Early access exceptions:** Severe financial hardship, terminal illness, total and permanent disability, temporary incapacity — these apply before preservation age in specific circumstances.

## Planning Around the Age 60 Threshold

For those born after 1964, turning 60 is the key milestone. Super withdrawals after 60 from a taxed fund are completely tax-free — no 15% tax on the taxable component that applies to withdrawals between preservation age and 60.

This tax-free status makes delaying super access until 60 financially advantageous even if you could access it at 59 under TTR.
