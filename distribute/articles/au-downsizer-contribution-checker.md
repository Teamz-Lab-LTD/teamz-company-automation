If you're 55 or older and selling a home you've owned for at least 10 years, you can contribute up to $300,000 from the proceeds into superannuation — regardless of your total super balance, work test, or non-concessional cap. This is the downsizer contribution, and it's one of the biggest last-chance super top-up opportunities in the Australian tax system.

## Who Qualifies?

All five conditions must be met:
1. You're 55 or older at the time of the contribution (age was reduced from 60 in July 2022, then 55 in 2023)
2. The property is in Australia and was your principal place of residence at some point
3. You or your spouse owned it for at least 10 years
4. You haven't previously made a downsizer contribution
5. You make the contribution within 90 days of settlement (extensions are possible but rare)

Couples can each contribute $300,000 — up to $600,000 combined — from the same property sale. The contribution must come from the sale proceeds, not other savings.

## How It Affects Your Super Balance

The downsizer contribution doesn't count toward the $110,000 non-concessional cap, and there's no requirement to be below the $1.9M transfer balance cap to make the contribution (though it will affect your pension phase later).

**Example: Couple sells $1.2M home, both aged 63**
- Each contributes $300,000 = $600,000 total into super
- Neither has exceeded transfer balance cap yet
- No work test required — both can contribute even if retired
- Earnings in super taxed at 15% (accumulation) vs marginal rate outside

## The 90-Day Window is the Most Common Mistake

The 90-day clock starts at settlement, not exchange. Many people miss the deadline because they think they have more time after moving in. The ATO does allow extensions in genuine cases (delay in settlement documents, health issues) but approval isn't guaranteed.
