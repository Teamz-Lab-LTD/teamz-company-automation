Financial advisors charge $200+/hour to build a personal balance sheet. But the concept is simple: list everything you own, subtract everything you owe, and you get your net worth. What matters is doing it consistently and knowing what the ratios mean.

The Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances (2022) shows median US net worth by age: $39,000 (under 35), $135,600 (35-44), $247,200 (45-54), $364,500 (55-64), $409,900 (65-74). Where do you stand?

## What the Calculator Tracks

**Assets** — organized into liquid (checking, savings, CDs), investments (brokerage, 401k/IRA, HSA), property (home, vehicles, real estate), and other (business interests, personal property).

**Liabilities** — mortgage, HELOC, auto loans, student loans, credit cards, personal loans, medical debt.

The tool calculates your total net worth plus two critical ratios: debt-to-asset ratio (how leveraged you are) and liquidity ratio (months of expenses covered by liquid assets). It also compares your net worth against the Federal Reserve median for your age group.

## Why Ratios Matter More Than Net Worth

A $500K net worth sounds great — until you realize $480K is locked in home equity and you have $2,000 in savings. Your liquidity ratio is terrible. The debt-to-asset ratio tells you how much of what you "own" is really the bank's. Financial planners recommend a liquidity ratio of 3-6 months and a debt-to-asset ratio under 0.50.
