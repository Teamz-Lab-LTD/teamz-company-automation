When you're too sick to work or you lose your job, Ireland's social insurance system provides two key short-term income supports: Illness Benefit and Jobseeker's Benefit. Both are based on your PRSI contribution record, and both have a standard personal rate plus increases for qualified adults and children. Getting the right figure upfront matters for planning.

## Illness Benefit Rates (2026)

Illness Benefit is paid when you're certified unfit for work by a GP for 6+ days. A 6-day waiting period applies before payment starts (days 1–6 not paid).

- **Personal rate:** €232/week
- **Increase for a Qualified Adult (IQA):** €154/week (spouse/civil partner earning under €310/week)
- **Increase for Qualified Child (IQC):** €46/week per child (full rate)

**Example: Ill worker, married, 2 children, spouse earns €200/week**
- Personal rate: €232
- IQA: €154 (spouse qualifies)
- IQC: €46 × 2 = €92
- Total: €478/week

Illness Benefit is taxable (income tax applies; USC does not). PRSI and pension contributions don't apply during Illness Benefit periods.

## Jobseeker's Benefit Rates (2026)

Same personal rate structure applies. Jobseeker's Benefit is paid when you're unemployed, available for work, and have enough PRSI contributions.

- **Personal rate:** €232/week
- **Duration:** 9 months if 260+ PRSI contributions paid; 6 months if 104–259 contributions

Jobseeker's Benefit is also taxable. If you exhaust it, Jobseeker's Allowance (means-tested, non-PRSI) may apply.

## PRSI Qualification Requirements

For both payments you need:
- Class A, H, or P PRSI contributions
- At least 104 contributions paid (not credited) over your working life
- At least 39 contributions in the Governing Contribution Year (2 years before current year)

Credited contributions (from other social welfare payments) count toward the 39 requirement but not the 104 total.
