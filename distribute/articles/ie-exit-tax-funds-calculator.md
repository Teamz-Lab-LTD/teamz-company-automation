If you invest in ETFs, unit-linked funds, or certain offshore funds in Ireland, you face one of the harshest investment tax regimes in the EU. A flat 41% exit tax applies to all gains and income — no 33% CGT rate, no €1,270 annual CGT exemption, and a unique 8-year deemed disposal rule that taxes unrealised gains even if you haven't sold anything.

## The 41% Exit Tax

Exit tax applies to gains from:
- Irish unit-linked life assurance funds
- Certain offshore funds (including EU/EEA UCITS ETFs bought through brokers)
- Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) — even passive index ETFs from Vanguard, iShares, etc.

The rate is 41% on the full gain, with no indexation relief, no annual exemption, and no CGT rate. Losses from exit-tax funds cannot be offset against CGT gains from shares.

**Example: €10,000 invested in a UCITS ETF, grows to €15,000 over 5 years**
- Gain: €5,000
- Exit tax at 41%: €2,050
- Net proceeds: €12,950
- Effective post-tax annual return reduced significantly vs Irish shares taxed at CGT 33%

## The 8-Year Deemed Disposal Rule

Every 8 years from the date of investment (not calendar year), Revenue deems you to have sold and immediately repurchased your holding at market value. You pay exit tax on the gain at that point — even with no actual sale.

**Example: €20,000 invested January 2018, grows to €35,000 by January 2026**
- 8-year anniversary: January 2026 → deemed disposal triggered
- Gain: €15,000 × 41% = €6,150 tax due, even though you haven't sold
- When you eventually sell, only gains after the last deemed disposal are taxed (avoids double taxation)

## Why This Discourages Long-Term ETF Investing in Ireland

The 8-year rule creates a compulsory "tax drag" every 8 years that significantly erodes compound growth compared to CGT-regime assets. Many Irish investors hold individual stocks precisely to avoid this.
