## What Is PITI — and Why Most Buyers Get Surprised

PITI stands for **Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance** — the four components that make up your *true* monthly housing cost. Not just the loan payment. The full bill.

Most first-time buyers focus on the principal and interest (P&I) number the bank quotes them and forget about the rest. That's how a $400,000 home at 6.75% can feel like a $2,336/month payment… until closing day, when the real number lands closer to **$2,985/month** once you add property taxes ($367), homeowners insurance ($117), and PMI ($165).

That $650/month gap is enough to blow a budget — or tank a mortgage approval.

## The 28% Front-End DTI Rule — Lenders Use This Every Time

When you apply for a mortgage, the first thing an underwriter does is calculate your **front-end debt-to-income ratio**: your total PITI divided by your gross monthly income.

Under conventional guidelines (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac), that ratio must stay at or below **28%**.

A few examples to make it concrete:

| Gross Monthly Income | Max PITI at 28% |
|---|---|
| $6,000 | $1,680 |
| $8,000 | $2,240 |
| $10,000 | $2,800 |
| $12,500 | $3,500 |

If your PITI exceeds 28%, lenders may still approve you with compensating factors — excellent credit (760+), large cash reserves, or a co-borrower. FHA loans allow up to 31% front-end. VA loans skip the percentage test entirely and use a residual income model instead.

## Breaking Down Each Component

Here's what goes into PITI — and what you can actually control:

**Principal & Interest** — the base amortization payment. Reducible by increasing your down payment, improving your credit score, or choosing a shorter term.

**Property Taxes** — collected monthly into escrow. The US national average is ~1.1% of home value per year, but it ranges from 0.28% (Hawaii) to 2.49% (New Jersey). Always verify your county rate — it has a permanent, compounding effect on your PITI.

**Homeowners Insurance** — required by all lenders. Rates vary 30–50% between carriers for the same property. Shopping around here is free money.

**PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)** — required on conventional loans when your down payment is less than 20%. It typically runs 0.2%–1.5% of the loan amount annually. The good news: under the Homeowners Protection Act, PMI must be cancelled automatically once your loan balance hits 78% of the original purchase price.

**HOA Fees** — not part of the traditional PITI acronym, but lenders count them in your debt-to-income ratio under a PITIA calculation. Common in condos and planned communities; US averages run $200–$400/month for condos.

## Practical Tips Before You Apply

- **Increase your down payment to 20%** to eliminate PMI entirely — on a $360K loan, that's ~$165/month saved indefinitely
- **Appeal your property tax assessment** if your home is over-assessed — 20–40% of appeals succeed according to industry data
- **Shop 3+ homeowners insurance quotes** before closing — bundling with auto insurance alone can cut 10–15%
- **A 40-point credit score increase** can drop your interest rate by 0.25–0.5%, saving $50–$100/month and lowering your PMI tier simultaneously
- **Use the 28% rule in reverse**: if you know your income, calculate your max PITI budget *before* you start browsing listings — it sets a hard ceiling and prevents emotional overspending
