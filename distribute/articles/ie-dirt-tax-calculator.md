Deposit Interest Retention Tax (DIRT) is automatically deducted by Irish banks before they pay you interest. The standard rate is 33%. Since 2020, PRSI at 4% also applies to deposit interest for most earners (self-assessed and employees over the PRSI threshold). Combined, that's 37% gone from your savings interest before you see it.

## How DIRT is Calculated

DIRT applies to gross interest earned on:
- Bank and credit union deposits
- Post Office savings certificates (Post Office Savings Bank)
- Irish Government Savings Bonds and Savings Certificates are DIRT-exempt

Banks deduct DIRT at source and remit to Revenue. You don't need to include it in your tax return unless you're exempt and want a refund (older adults, low income) or owe additional PRSI.

**Example: €50,000 in a fixed-term deposit at 3% gross interest**
- Gross interest: €1,500
- DIRT (33%): €495
- PRSI (4%): €60
- Net interest received: €945 (effective rate: 1.89%)

This is why chasing a 3% gross rate isn't as attractive as it first appears — the after-DIRT-and-PRSI yield is under 2%.

## DIRT Exemptions

You can claim a full DIRT refund if:
- You're over 65 (or jointly assessed with a spouse/civil partner who is)
- Your total income is below the income tax exemption threshold (€36,000 for a married couple, €18,000 for single person 2026)
- You're permanently incapacitated

First-time buyers can also claim a DIRT refund on savings interest earned in the 4 years before purchasing a home.

## PRSI on Savings: New Since 2020

Before 2020, PRSI didn't apply to deposit interest. The 4% charge now applies to individuals in Classes A and S (employees and self-employed). Those over pension age who are PRSI-exempt are not affected.
