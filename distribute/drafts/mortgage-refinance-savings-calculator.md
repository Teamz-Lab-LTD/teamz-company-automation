# Should You Refinance Your Mortgage in 2026? This Free Calculator Shows Exactly How Much You'd Save

With mortgage rates hovering around 6.5–7% after the Federal Reserve held the federal funds rate at 4.25–4.50% through March 2026, millions of homeowners are asking: **is refinancing worth it right now?**

The answer depends on three numbers: your current rate, your new rate, and your closing costs. Our [Mortgage Refinance Savings Calculator](https://tool.teamzlab.com/mortgage/refinance-savings-calculator/) does the math instantly.

## What the Calculator Shows You

Enter your current loan balance, interest rate, remaining term, and the new rate you've been quoted. The calculator returns:

- **Monthly payment reduction** — the immediate cash flow improvement
- **Break-even month** — when cumulative savings exceed closing costs
- **Total interest saved** — often $20,000–$80,000 over the loan life
- **Side-by-side cost comparison** — remaining current loan vs. new loan with closing costs

## When Refinancing Makes Sense

The classic rule: refinance when you can drop your rate by at least 0.75–1.0 percentage points AND plan to stay in the home past the break-even point. With typical closing costs of 2–5% of the loan amount (source: [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov)), a $300,000 refi at $9,000 in costs needs roughly 18–24 months to break even on a 1% rate drop.

## The 2026 Rate Environment

The OBBB Act (P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) stabilized the tax landscape, but mortgage rates remain elevated versus the 2021 lows. Homeowners who locked in 2.5–3.5% rates during 2020–2021 likely shouldn't refinance. But those with 7.5%+ rates from 2023 purchases may find significant savings as rates moderate. Source: [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov), [Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com).
