Fringe Benefit Tax (FBT) in New Zealand is paid by the employer — not the employee — on non-cash benefits provided to staff. The standard rate sounds alarming: 63.93% of the taxable value of the benefit. But that rate is designed to be equivalent to the top personal income tax rate on the grossed-up benefit, so employees pay nothing directly. Employers need to get the calculation right to avoid underpaying or structuring benefits inefficiently.

## Why the Rate is 63.93%

The 63.93% rate is a mathematical consequence of the 39% top marginal income tax rate. If you give an employee a benefit worth $100, and they would have received it as salary (and paid 39% tax), the equivalent gross salary would be $163.93 to net $100. The FBT of 63.93% × $100 = $63.93 equals the tax that would have been paid.

Alternative FBT rates:
- **49.25%** — if the employee's income is $14,000–$57,600 (equivalent to 33% marginal rate)
- **43.17%** — if $57,600–$78,100 (30% rate band, rarely applicable now)
- **63.93%** — top rate, applies to all employees earning $78,100+

Employers can choose to apply a flat 63.93% to all employees (simpler) or use the applicable rate per employee.

## Most Common FBT Benefits

**Motor vehicles:** FBT on company cars is 20% of the cost price per year (21.43% for older vehicles), applied quarterly. A $50,000 car costs the employer $10,000/year in FBT alone.

**Car parks:** If you provide a car park in a restricted parking area, FBT applies to the market value of the park.

**Low-interest loans:** The difference between the prescribed FBT interest rate and the rate charged to the employee is a taxable benefit.

**Entertainment:** Business entertainment above certain thresholds triggers FBT if attributable to specific employees.
