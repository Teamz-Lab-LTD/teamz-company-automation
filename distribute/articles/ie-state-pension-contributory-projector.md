Ireland's State Pension (Contributory) moved from the old Yearly Average method to the Total Contributions Approach (TCA) from 1 January 2024. The TCA pays a pension proportional to how many PRSI contributions you've made in your lifetime — with a maximum of 2,080 contributions (40 years at 52/year) giving the full rate. This is a fairer system, but it rewards people who understand it and top up gaps.

## How TCA Works

Full State Pension (Contributory) 2026: €277.30/week (personal rate)

Your pension = (Contributions ÷ 2,080) × €277.30/week

**Example: 1,560 paid contributions (30 years of work)**
- 1,560 ÷ 2,080 = 75%
- Weekly pension: 75% × €277.30 = €207.98

**Example: 2,080 contributions (40 years)**
- Full rate: €277.30/week = €14,420/year

HomeCaring Periods: Up to 20 years of caring for children or incapacitated adults can be credited toward the 2,080 target. These "HomeCaring Credits" replaced the old Home Makers Scheme.

## Filling the Gaps: Voluntary Contributions

If you fall short of 2,080 by state pension age (currently 66), you can make Voluntary PRSI contributions if:
- You're under 70
- You've made at least 520 PRSI contributions previously
- You applied within 60 months of leaving insurable employment

The cost of voluntary contributions is approximately €641/year (Class S equivalent), and each year adds ~52 contributions toward the 2,080 total.

## Deferring State Pension

Since 2024, you can defer the State Pension beyond 66, up to age 70. Each deferred year increases the weekly rate by approximately 4-6% (the exact actuarial uplift is recalculated annually). Deferring 4 years could increase your pension from €277 to ~€310+/week for life.
