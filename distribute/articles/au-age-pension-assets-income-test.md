Centrelink applies two separate tests to determine your Age Pension eligibility — the assets test and the income test — and pays you based on whichever produces the lower pension. Most people focus on one and ignore the other, and end up either missing out on entitlements or planning incorrectly for retirement.

## How the Two Tests Work

**Assets test (2026 thresholds):**
- Single homeowner: full pension up to $295,500 in assets; cuts out at $695,500
- Couple homeowner: full pension up to $451,500; cuts out at $1,045,500
- Non-homeowners get $232,500 higher thresholds in each band

For every $1,000 in assets over the lower threshold, the fortnightly pension reduces by $3.

**Income test:**
- Single: full pension up to $204/fortnight; cuts out at $2,444.60/fortnight
- Couple combined: full pension up to $360/fortnight; cuts out at $3,737.60/fortnight

For every $1 of income over the free area, pension reduces by 50 cents.

## The Trap: Deeming Rules on Financial Assets

Cash, term deposits, shares, and managed funds are subject to deeming — Centrelink assumes they earn a set rate regardless of actual returns. In 2026: 0.25% on the first $62,600 (single) and 2.25% above that. This means a $500K share portfolio "earns" about $9,900 deemed income per year even if dividends are lower.

**Example: Single homeowner with $400K in super + $200K home contents:**
- Assets test: $400K < $695,500 cutout → partial pension applies
- Income test: deemed income on $400K = ~$7,800/year → ~$5.19/fortnight reduction
- Final pension: determined by the test producing the lower rate

## Why Both Tests Matter at Once

Some people are well within the assets test but fail the income test due to rental income or a pension from overseas. Others have minimal income but large assets. You need to run both calculations simultaneously to find your actual entitlement.
