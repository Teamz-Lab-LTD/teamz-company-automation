Maternity Benefit in Ireland pays 80% of your gross average weekly earnings from the previous tax year — subject to a minimum of €262/week and a maximum of €450/week. Paternity Benefit pays the same rate but for just 2 weeks. Neither benefit is your employer's responsibility to top up (though many do), and knowing the exact amount helps you plan the months ahead.

## Maternity Benefit: The Calculation

Weekly rate = 80% × average gross weekly earnings in the relevant tax year (the second-last complete tax year before your baby's expected birth).

For a birth in 2026, the relevant tax year is 2024. The Department of Social Protection (DSPĀ) uses your P60 or Revenue record for 2024.

**Example: Gross salary 2024 = €54,600 (weekly: €1,050)**
- 80% of €1,050 = €840/week → but capped at €450/week
- Maternity Benefit = €450/week × 26 weeks = €11,700

**Example: Gross salary 2024 = €30,000 (weekly: €576.92)**
- 80% = €461.54 → capped at €450/week
- Maternity Benefit = €450/week × 26 weeks = €11,700

**Example: Gross salary 2024 = €24,000 (weekly: €461.54)**
- 80% = €369.23 → below cap
- Maternity Benefit = €369.23/week × 26 weeks = €9,600

The minimum €262/week ensures those with irregular earnings still receive meaningful support.

## PRSI Contributions Requirement

To qualify you must have:
- At least 39 PRSI contributions in the 12 months before your expected week of confinement, OR
- At least 39 contributions paid at any time + 39 in the second-last tax year

Class A, E, and H workers qualify. Class M (low-income earners not paying PRSI) do not.

## Paternity Benefit

Same calculation method, same min/max, but 2 weeks only. Must be claimed within 26 weeks of birth. Also available for same-sex couples and adoptive parents.

## Parent's Benefit (Extended Leave)

Both parents can claim an additional 9 weeks of Parent's Benefit (paid at the same rate) in the first 2 years after birth. This extends beyond maternity/paternity leave and can be taken flexibly.
