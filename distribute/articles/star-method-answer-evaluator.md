# Is Your Interview Answer Good Enough? Score It with the STAR Method

You've prepared your behavioral interview answers. But how do you know if they're actually good? Most candidates practice answers in their head without any objective feedback on structure, specificity, or impact.

The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the gold standard for behavioral interview answers. Interviewers are specifically trained to listen for these four components — and they notice when one is missing.

## Common STAR Mistakes

1. **Too much Situation, not enough Action** — spending 60% of your answer setting context instead of describing what *you* did
2. **"We" instead of "I"** — interviewers want to know your contribution, not the team's
3. **No quantified Results** — "it went well" vs "reduced processing time by 35%"
4. **Too long or too short** — ideal length is 150-300 words (60-90 seconds spoken)
5. **Missing the Task** — jumping from Situation to Action without stating what was expected of you

## What Makes a Strong Answer

- **Specific context**: names, dates, project scope — not "at my previous company"
- **Clear ownership**: "I designed", "I led", "I proposed" — first-person singular
- **Measurable results**: percentages, dollar amounts, time saved, team size managed
- **Balanced proportion**: ~15% Situation, ~15% Task, ~50% Action, ~20% Result
