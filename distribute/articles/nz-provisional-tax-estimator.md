If you're self-employed or earn income that isn't taxed at source in New Zealand, you pay provisional tax — tax on income you haven't yet earned (in theory). The standard method is the uplift method: pay 105% of last year's residual income tax in three instalments. Get it wrong and you face use-of-money interest (UOMI) charges at 10.39% per annum.

## The 105% Uplift Method Explained

Residual income tax (RIT) = income tax for the year minus PAYE withheld and other tax credits.

If your RIT was $12,000 last year:
- 105% × $12,000 = $12,600 provisional tax due this year
- Paid in 3 instalments: $4,200 on 28 August, $4,200 on 15 January, $4,200 on 7 May

This is safe harbour — if you pay via uplift method, no UOMI applies even if your actual tax turns out higher.

## Alternative: Estimation Method

If you expect your income to be significantly lower than last year (e.g., after losing a major client), you can estimate your actual RIT and pay accordingly. Risk: if you underestimate by more than $60,000 or by more than 20%, UOMI applies to the shortfall.

**Example: Last year RIT = $18,000, this year income expected to drop 40%**
- Uplift method: $18,900 (105% × $18,000) — safe but overpays by ~$7,000
- Estimation method: $11,000 (estimated actual) — saves $7,900 cashflow but requires accuracy

## UOMI Rates

If you underpay by more than the safe harbour amount, IRD charges UOMI at 10.39% per annum (2026 rate) on the shortfall from the due date. On a $10,000 shortfall over 3 months, that's ~$260 — painful but not catastrophic for short periods.

## GST Interaction

Provisional tax is separate from GST. If you're GST-registered with 2-monthly filing, your GST return months often overlap with provisional tax months — budgeting for both simultaneously is essential.
