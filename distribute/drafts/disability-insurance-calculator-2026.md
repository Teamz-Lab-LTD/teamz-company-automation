# How Much Disability Insurance Do You Actually Need?

One in four workers will experience a disability before age 67 (source: [Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov)). Yet most Americans either have no disability coverage or rely solely on employer plans that replace only 60% of salary — and that 60% is taxable, netting closer to 45%.

## Why Employer Coverage Isn't Enough

The "life insurance calculator" keyword cluster gets 8,100 monthly searches at $16.18 CPC (Google Ads data, April 2026). Disability insurance searches are growing faster because people are discovering the gap: employer-paid disability benefits are **taxable income**. If your employer covers 60% of your $80,000 salary ($4,000/month), you net roughly $3,000 after federal tax at the 25% bracket. If your expenses are $3,500/month, you're $500/month short — every month, potentially for years.

Individual disability policies, by contrast, pay benefits **tax-free** because you paid premiums with after-tax dollars. The calculator models this tax distinction automatically.

## Short-Term vs Long-Term Disability

Short-term disability (STD) covers 3–6 months. Long-term disability (LTD) kicks in after the elimination period (typically 90 days) and can pay to age 65. The calculator lets you choose benefit periods of 2 years, 5 years, or to age 65, and adjusts premium estimates accordingly.

## Who Needs This Most

- High-income professionals whose employer plan is capped
- Single-income households with no spouse backup
- Anyone with mortgage payments and children
