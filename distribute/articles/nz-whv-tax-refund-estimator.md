Tens of thousands of working holidaymakers leave New Zealand every year without claiming their tax refund. The average refund is $NZ 500–$2,500, and the process is simpler than most people expect — you just need your final payslips, your IRD number, and to file a departing New Zealand tax return. This tool estimates what you're owed before you go through the process.

## Why Working Holiday Workers Are Typically Owed a Refund

Most WHV workers are employed using the M (primary) tax code, which assumes you'll earn income year-round. Since most WHV workers only work part of the tax year (April–March in NZ), their actual annual income is lower than what the PAYE system assumed — and they've overpaid tax. The refund is the difference.

**Example: Worked 7 months, earned $28,000 total**
- PAYE system assumed $28,000 = full-year income → deducted tax at rate for that band
- Actual annualised income: $28,000 (7 months → $48,000/year equivalent)
- But actual income is only $28,000 → falls in lower tax band
- Tax paid: ~$4,200 (at 17.5%)
- Tax owed on $28,000: ~$3,780
- Estimated refund: ~$420 + any overpaid ACC levy

Tip: If you were taxed at S or SB (secondary income codes), overpayment is usually larger.

## What You Need to File

1. Your IRD number (issued when you arrived and started working)
2. Total gross income from all NZ employers (from payslips or myIR)
3. Total PAYE deducted (from payslips)
4. Your overseas bank account details (IRD can refund to overseas accounts)

You can file through myIR online or by paper IR3 form. IRD aims to process departing resident refunds within 6–8 weeks.

## Can You Claim After Leaving NZ?

Yes — you have 4 years to claim a tax refund from the end of the relevant tax year. Most WHV workers can still claim for multiple past years. One tax agent or direct myIR filing covers all years at once.
