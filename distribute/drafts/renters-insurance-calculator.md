Most renters assume their landlord's insurance covers their stuff. It doesn't. If a fire, burst pipe, or break-in hits your apartment, your landlord's policy covers the building walls — not your laptop, furniture, or wardrobe.

The average American renter owns $20,000–$50,000 worth of personal property. Yet only 55% carry renters insurance, according to the Insurance Information Institute (iii.org).

## Replacement Cost vs Actual Cash Value

The biggest decision in any renters policy: replacement cost pays today's price for a new equivalent item. Actual cash value (ACV) deducts depreciation. That 5-year-old laptop worth $1,200 new? Under ACV, you might only get $400. Replacement cost premiums run 10–20% higher but the payout difference at claim time is dramatic.

## What Smart Renters Do

- Bundle with auto insurance for a 5–15% discount
- Choose a $1,000 deductible to drop premiums 15–25%
- Document belongings with photos (stored in cloud, not on the device you're insuring)
- Add scheduled personal property riders for jewelry over $1,500
