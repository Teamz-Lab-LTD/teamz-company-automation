# Self-Directed IRA vs Traditional IRA: Is Alternative Investing Worth the Fees?

A Self-Directed IRA (SDIRA) lets you invest in assets that traditional brokerages don't offer: real estate, precious metals, private equity, tax liens, promissory notes, and even cryptocurrency. But is the added complexity and higher fees worth it?

## What Makes SDIRA Different

A regular IRA limits you to stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and ETFs. An SDIRA gives you access to virtually any investment the IRS allows — which is everything *except* life insurance, collectibles, and S-Corp stock.

The catch: you need a specialized custodian, and they charge more. Annual fees typically range from $200-$500+, plus transaction fees for each investment.

## The Fee Drag Problem

On a $100,000 SDIRA with $400/year in custodian fees earning 8% returns over 20 years:

- **Without fees**: $466,096
- **With fees**: $449,462
- **Fee drag**: $16,634

That's real money. If your alternative investments aren't outperforming traditional index funds by at least 0.5-1% annually, you're paying extra for the privilege of underperforming.

## UBIT: The Hidden Tax

If your SDIRA invests in leveraged real estate (using a mortgage), the income attributable to the financed portion triggers UBIT (Unrelated Business Income Tax). This can be 10-37% on the leveraged portion — effectively eliminating the tax advantage on that income.

## When SDIRA Makes Sense

- You have expertise in real estate or alternative investments
- Your expected returns significantly exceed public market returns
- You understand prohibited transaction rules (no self-dealing!)
- You can absorb the higher custodian fees
