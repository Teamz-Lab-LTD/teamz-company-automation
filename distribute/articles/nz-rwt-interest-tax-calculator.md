Resident Withholding Tax (RWT) is deducted by your bank before paying you interest on savings, term deposits, and bonds. The RWT rate you register with your bank determines how much is withheld — and if you register the wrong rate, you either overpay throughout the year or face an unexpected tax shortfall at filing time.

## RWT Rates on Interest Income

| Taxable income | RWT rate |
|---------------|----------|
| Up to $14,000 | 10.5% |
| $14,001–$48,000 | 17.5% |
| $48,001–$70,000 | 30% |
| Over $70,000 | 33% |
| No IRD number provided | 45% (default penalty rate) |

The RWT rate should match your marginal income tax rate. If you don't register a rate with your bank, they automatically deduct 33% — even if your marginal rate is 17.5%, meaning you'll need to reclaim the excess via your tax return.

**Example: $80,000 in a 3-month rolling term deposit at 5.2%, income = $65,000**
- Gross interest: $4,160
- Correct RWT rate: 30%
- RWT deducted: $1,248
- Net interest received: $2,912

If wrong rate (33%) was applied: $1,373 deducted → $125 overpaid → refundable at filing.

## Non-Resident Withholding Tax (NRWT)

For non-resident depositors, NRWT applies instead — typically 15% under most tax treaties. If you're a New Zealand tax resident but your bank has incorrectly set NRWT, the rates are wrong.

## When Does RWT Not Apply?

- Interest paid to companies (different rules)
- Interest under $50 total from a single payer per year (exemption)
- Government bonds held directly in some cases
