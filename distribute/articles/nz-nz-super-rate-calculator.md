New Zealand Superannuation (NZ Super) is a universal pension paid from age 65 — no means test, no asset test. The amount you receive depends on your living situation (single, couple, living alone vs sharing) and your tax code. Getting both right means you receive exactly what you're entitled to without underpaying or overpaying tax throughout the year.

## NZ Super Rates by Living Situation (2026)

| Living situation | Gross per week | Gross per year |
|-----------------|---------------|----------------|
| Single, living alone | $496 | $25,792 |
| Single, sharing accommodation | $458 | $23,816 |
| Couple (each) | $381 | $19,812 |
| Couple, one partner not eligible | $762 (both) | $39,624 |

These rates are reviewed annually in April. The April 2026 review typically adjusts for wage growth.

## Choosing the Right Tax Code

NZ Super is taxable income. Your tax code determines how much PAYE is deducted at source by Work and Income:

- **M:** Standard tax code — use if NZ Super is your only income or main income. If you have no other income under $14,000 → effective rate is 10.5%.
- **M SL:** M code + student loan repayment
- **S:** Secondary income code — use if you have another job or income where M/ME is already applied
- **SB / S / SH / ST:** Secondary income codes for different income bands

**Why it matters:** Using the wrong tax code leads to a tax bill in April or under-deduction. If you also have rental income, an employer job, or partnership income, you likely need a secondary code on NZ Super.

## Overseas Pension Deduction

If you receive a pension from another country and it's deducted from NZ Super under a social security agreement (UK, Australia, Netherlands, etc.), your NZ Super reduces by the overseas pension amount.
