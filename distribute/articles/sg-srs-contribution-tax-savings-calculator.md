The Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS) is Singapore's voluntary top-up retirement account with an immediate tax benefit: every dollar you contribute reduces your chargeable income dollar-for-dollar. For a Singapore PR or Citizen earning above $80,000, a maximum SRS contribution saves over $2,000 in tax every year — guaranteed, before any investment returns.

## SRS Contribution Limits and Tax Rates

Annual contribution limits (2026):
- Singapore Citizens and PRs: $15,300
- Foreigners: $35,700

Singapore's income tax is progressive. At $80,000 chargeable income the marginal rate is 11.5%; at $120,000 it's 15%; at $160,000 it's 18%. The SRS deduction reduces income from the top bracket.

**Example: Chargeable income $120,000, max SRS contribution $15,300**
- Tax without SRS: $120,000 income → ~$11,040
- Tax with SRS: $104,700 income → ~$8,750
- Saving: ~$2,290 per year
- Over 20 years at the same income: ~$45,800 in tax avoided (before investment returns)

## The Retirement Withdrawal Bonus

At 62 (statutory retirement age), only 50% of SRS withdrawals are taxable. If you've retired by then and have little other income, the effective tax rate on SRS withdrawals could be 0–4% — compared to 11–18% you saved at contribution. This asymmetry is the core advantage.

You have 10 years after the first withdrawal to draw down the account. Spreading $200,000 over 10 years = $10,000 taxable income per year — well within the zero-tax band if you have no other Singapore income.

## Who Benefits Most?

SRS delivers the highest return for:
- Those earning $80,000+ (11.5% marginal rate or above)
- Those planning to retire in Singapore and draw down SRS in lower income years
- Foreigners with the higher $35,700 cap and temporary higher income

It's less useful if you're near retirement with limited years to save, or if you expect high income in retirement.
