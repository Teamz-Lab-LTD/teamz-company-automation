Portfolio Investment Entities (PIEs) — including KiwiSaver funds and many managed funds — tax you at your Prescribed Investor Rate (PIR), not your marginal income tax rate. The PIR is based on your taxable income from the past two income years, with three possible rates: 10.5%, 17.5%, or 28%. Choosing the wrong PIR means either overpaying tax or facing a claw-back bill from IRD.

## How Your PIR is Determined

Your PIR is the lower of:
1. Your marginal income tax rate on total taxable income
2. The rate based on your PIE income specifically

| Taxable income (last 2 years) | PIR |
|------------------------------|-----|
| Up to $14,000 | 10.5% |
| $14,001 – $48,000 | 17.5% |
| Over $48,000 | 28% |

The rule: you use the lower of the two years' thresholds. If your income was $40,000 one year and $55,000 the next, you'd use the 17.5% PIR (the year under $48,000 sets the floor).

**Example: Income Year 1: $35,000, Year 2: $52,000**
- Year 1: falls in 17.5% band
- Year 2: falls in 28% band
- PIR = 17.5% (the lower of the two applicable rates)

## Why PIR Matters in KiwiSaver

For a KiwiSaver fund earning 5% on $100,000:
- $5,000 return at 28% PIR = $3,600 net
- $5,000 return at 17.5% PIR = $4,125 net
- Difference: $525/year — compounding over 10 years is over $7,000

If you're eligible for 17.5% but your provider has 28% on file, you're overpaying by $525/year. You can reclaim overpaid PIE tax through your end-of-year tax assessment, but you need to proactively set the right rate with your PIE provider.

## Changing Your PIR

Notify your PIE provider (KiwiSaver fund manager, bank, fund) directly — IRD doesn't set it automatically. You should review your PIR at the start of each tax year (April) after lodging your income return.
