Your parents own a modest three-bedroom house in the South East, a pension pot, and some savings. Nothing extravagant — yet their estate is now comfortably above the inheritance tax threshold. With UK house prices averaging £290,000 nationally and considerably more in London, an increasing number of ordinary families are being pulled into the IHT net. ## How UK Inheritance Tax Works

IHT is charged at 40% on the value of an estate above the nil-rate band (NRB) of £325,000. This threshold has been frozen since 2009 and will remain frozen until at least April 2028 under the Finance Act 2021.

Two additional reliefs can significantly reduce the bill:

**Residence nil-rate band (RNRB): £175,000**
Available when a home is left to direct descendants (children, grandchildren). This effectively raises the tax-free threshold to £500,000 for an individual. However, the RNRB tapers away for estates worth more than £2 million — reducing by £1 for every £2 above that threshold.

**Spouse/civil partner exemption: unlimited**
Assets passing to a surviving spouse are entirely exempt. The unused NRB and RNRB transfer to the surviving spouse, meaning a married couple can pass up to £1 million completely tax-free.

**Example — Single person, estate £750,000 (includes home):**
- NRB: −£325,000
- RNRB (home to children): −£175,000
- Taxable estate: £250,000
- IHT at 40%: **£100,000**

Without the RNRB (e.g., no direct descendants, or home not left to them): the bill jumps to £170,000.

## The 7-Year Gift Rule and Taper Relief

Gifts made more than 7 years before death are completely exempt from IHT. Gifts made within 7 years are "potentially exempt transfers" (PETs) and may be taxed on a sliding scale:

| Years before death | Tax rate on gift |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | 40% |
| 3–4 | 32% |
| 4–5 | 24% |
| 5–6 | 16% |
| 6–7 | 8% |
| 7+ | 0% |

Taper relief only applies when the cumulative value of gifts exceeds the NRB. Annual gifts up to £3,000 per year are always exempt (and unused allowance carries forward one year). Small gifts of £250 per person, wedding gifts (£5,000 to a child, £2,500 to a grandchild), and gifts from surplus income are also exempt.

## The Pension IHT Trap from April 2027

Currently, unspent pension pots pass outside the estate for IHT purposes. The Autumn Budget 2024 announced that from April 2027, unused pension funds will be brought within the scope of IHT. For someone with a £400,000 pension and a £600,000 estate, this could create a new IHT liability of around £160,000 that doesn't exist today.

This makes pension drawdown strategy — when and how much to withdraw — a critical part of estate planning from 2027 onward.
