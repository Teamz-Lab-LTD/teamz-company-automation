# CPF OA vs Mortgage: Should You Use CPF to Pay Your Home Loan?

Every Singaporean homeowner faces this question: should you use your CPF Ordinary Account to pay your mortgage, or keep it earning 2.5% interest and pay from cash?

The answer isn't as obvious as it seems. While using CPF OA reduces your monthly cash outflow, there's a hidden cost most people overlook: **accrued interest**.

## The Accrued Interest Trap

When you use CPF OA for housing, CPB Board tracks the amount withdrawn plus 2.5% compound interest. When you sell the property, you must repay this full amount (principal + accrued interest) back to your CPF account — before you pocket any profit.

After 25 years on a $400,000 withdrawal, accrued interest alone can reach $250,000+. That's money "owed" back to your own CPF account, reducing your cash proceeds from the sale.

## When CPF OA Makes Sense

- Your mortgage rate is **higher than 2.5%** (currently most floating rates are 3-4%)
- You don't have sufficient cash savings as emergency fund
- You're buying an HDB flat (CPF usage is more straightforward)

## When Cash Payment Makes Sense

- Your mortgage rate is **below 2.5%** (rare but possible with fixed-rate promos)
- You plan to hold the property long-term (accrued interest compounds aggressively)
- You want maximum retirement adequacy (CPF OA balance counts toward Basic Retirement Sum)
